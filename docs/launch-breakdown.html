Sheet 01Built

What has been built

A complete, self-contained website with an editor attached to it. Nothing below is outstanding work — it is finished and running.

The site itself Built

  • HomeThe setting-out drawing, the Landscaping / Alatu diptych, and the five-project index list.
  • WorkThe portfolio grid, carrying all seven projects with sheet numbering and a title block.
  • Project page — Poolside gardenThe full case-study template, including the before / after slider. One project is written; six are waiting on content.
  • FlowersSupply-and-install gallery, ten picture slots laid out and captioned.
  • CareThe Alatu maintenance offer — three plans, inverted to ink for the whole page.
  • StudioAbout, process and the contact form.
  • 404A branded not-found page, so a mistyped link still looks like the firm.

The systems underneath

  • A design system, not page-by-page stylingEvery colour, type size and spacing step is defined once. Eighteen reusable components. Changing a colour changes it everywhere, correctly.
  • An editor for the businessA visual admin covering projects, flowers, care plans and contact details — no code, no developer. Written up in docs/EDITING.md.
  • The rules are enforced by the buildIf the home page ever has six projects instead of five, or a photograph is uploaded without a description, the site refuses to publish and says which one. A failed build is cheaper than a broken site.
  • Speed on a Nigerian mobile connectionAbout 12 KB over the wire for a first visit. No framework is sent to the browser. Nothing moves on the page as it loads.
  • Contrast worked to WCAG AAThree colours were adjusted to pass, with the arithmetic recorded in docs/CONTRAST.md.
  • Enquiries route to WhatsApp with no serverThe contact form composes a structured message and opens WhatsApp. Nothing to host, nothing to hack, no spam, and the enquiry arrives readable instead of “hi how much for garden”.
  • Business details published for machines tooName, address, hours and service area are marked up so Google and WhatsApp read them consistently.
  • Deployment committed to the repositoryThe hosting configuration lives in the project, so the live site can be rebuilt from scratch by anyone, with no clicking through a dashboard.

Sheet 04Advise

Four things we recommend before launch

Found while preparing this handover. Each is small work, and each affects how the site performs in the one situation it was designed for — someone was given the name and sent the link.

  • A link-preview image RecommendedWhen the link is shared on WhatsApp today, it arrives as text with no picture. The site is set up to show one; the image itself has never been made. This matters more here than on most sites, because WhatsApp is the main way the link will travel.
  • Analytics RecommendedNothing is currently measuring visits or WhatsApp tap-throughs, so there will be no way to tell whether the site is working. Cloudflare Web Analytics is free and adds a single line to the site.
  • Take the paid hosting plan from day one RecommendedOn the free plan the site sleeps after 15 minutes without visitors, and the next person waits about a minute for it to wake — and that person is precisely the referred client the site exists to impress. The paid plan at about ₦11,000 a month removes it entirely, and is the figure carried in sheet 03. It is the one running cost worth paying without hesitation.
  • Lock the contact details once they are real RecommendedOnce the live number is in, a single setting on the host makes it impossible to ever publish the placeholder again by accident.

One decision left in the design

The specification asks for the Work page in two different forms — a card grid in one section, a text index in another. It is built as the card grid, because that is what the page specification and the drawn screens both show. Worth a look and a decision, but it does not block launch.

Sheet 05Sequence

The launch sequence

In order. Steps 1 to 5 can be done in an afternoon once the details are to hand; the rest is content, and can follow the site being live.

  1. Confirm the domain nameKeep kpomey.com or choose another. Everything downstream assumes it.
  2. Buy the domainThe only compulsory payment. Renew yearly, ideally with auto-renew on — a lapsed domain takes the site and the email down together.
  3. Open the mailbox and set up hello@Zoho free tier or Google Workspace. Point it at the new domain.
  4. Give us the live WhatsApp number and emailThey go into one settings screen and update every button on every page at once.
  5. PublishConnect the host, attach the domain, and the certificate issues itself. Then open it on a phone and press Send on WhatsApp — that is the one test nothing else covers.
  6. Send the logoThe header mark, the browser icon and the link preview all come from it.
  7. Send photographsOriginals, straight from the phone or camera — the site compresses them itself, and better. Priority: the poolside garden before-and-after pair, then one strong shot per project, then flowers.
  8. Decide the care pricesThen they can be typed straight into the editor.
  9. Write up the remaining projectsOne at a time. Each becomes a page the moment its scope and facts are written; until then it still appears on Work as a picture and a caption.
  10. Claim the Google Business ProfileFree, and the highest-return thing on this list after the site itself.
Sheet 06After

Running it afterwards

What you can change yourself

Contact details, projects, flower photographs and care prices — all through the editor, with no developer and no code. The one-page guide is docs/EDITING.md, written for whoever will actually do it.

What is deliberately locked

Layout, colours, typefaces, the Landscaping / Alatu spread, the sheet numbers and the order of sections. That is the point: those decisions are what make the site look like it was made by a firm rather than assembled, and an editor that could change them eventually would. Everything that should change month to month is editable.

Editing from a phone

The editor currently runs on a computer that has the project on it. To open it from a phone or any browser, it needs to be connected to the code repository and deployed as its own small site — a short piece of work, still free to host. Worth doing if the person updating the site is not at a desk.

Ongoing attention

  • YearlyDomain renewal. Set auto-renew and it is nothing.
  • Monthly, if you choose itHosting and mailbox, if you take the paid tiers.
  • Every jobThree photographs — before, during, after, from the same standing position. That habit is what keeps the portfolio growing without a shoot ever being commissioned.
